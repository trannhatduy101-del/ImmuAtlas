Our mission

ImmuAtlas helps the public explore reliable information about vaccination and preventable infectious diseases — with the context and denominators needed to read every figure honestly.

How to use this site

Six pages, three levels of depth. Follow them in order the first time; after that, jump straight to the one that answers your question.

  1. Start on Home

    Four figures set the scope before you read anything else: how many countries report, which years the data covers, how many doses were given, and how many cases were recorded.

  2. Open Explore → Vaccination rates

    Choose an antigen and a year, and narrow to a country and/or a region. Read the regional table first for context, then the country table below it for the detail. Picking a single antigen also brings in that disease’s herd immunity threshold, so each figure can be read against a target instead of on its own.

  3. Open Explore → Infections by economic status

    Choose one economic group, one disease and one year. The Flagged count shows how many countries in the group reported zero in every year — those stay in the totals, but you can see how many there are.

  4. Open Compare → Biggest improvement

    Choose an antigen plus a start year and an end year. The # column ranks countries by how much coverage rose, and that rank is fixed — re-sorting the table moves the rows around but never renumbers them.

  5. Open Compare → Above the global rate

    Choose a year and a disease. The Global row sits at the top of the table; every row beneath it reported a higher rate than that single global figure.

  6. Take the data with you

    Every result table has CSV and PDF buttons in its header. A download always contains the whole filtered result, never just the page you are looking at.

FAQ

Quick answers to help you understand how ImmuAtlas works and how to interpret its results.

What is ImmuAtlas? ⌄

ImmuAtlas is an interactive site for exploring vaccination coverage and preventable infectious disease data. It helps users compare countries, regions, economic groups and years while keeping the population context behind each result visible.

How should I choose a page? ⌄

Start with the Home page for an overview, then use Vaccination Rates to explore coverage, Infections by Economic Status to compare economic groups, Biggest Improvement to examine changes in coverage, or Above the Global Rate to identify countries with higher infection rates than the global figure.

What does an infection rate per 100,000 mean? ⌄

It shows reported cases relative to population size, standardised to 100,000 people. This makes infection levels easier to compare between countries or groups with different population sizes.

Why are some countries shown as having zero reported cases? ⌄

A reported zero does not necessarily mean that no infections occurred. In some cases, the available data shows zero across the series, so ImmuAtlas flags these records to remind users that a reported zero and an unreported year may look the same in the source data.

Can I download the results I find? ⌄

Yes. Result tables provide CSV and PDF download options. Downloads contain the complete filtered result, so you can keep or analyse the data beyond the page view.
