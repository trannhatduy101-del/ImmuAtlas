How this ranking was built

Coverage
The share of its target group a country reached with {{ antigen_name }}, as reported to WHO. The change is the end year figure minus the start year figure, measured in percentage points.
Who is ranked
A country appears only if it reported a figure in both {{ start_year }} and {{ end_year }}; {{ fmt_int(eligible) }} did. Without both years a country could appear to improve simply because its earlier figure is missing. The rank is always by coverage gain, so it stays with a country whatever the table is sorted by.
Doses per 100 people
Doses divided by the whole population rather than the target group. The figure appears in the CSV and PDF.
Coverage above 100%
A country can deliver more doses than there are children in its target group, so some reported figures pass 100%. The increase is measured on the figures as reported, never on a capped version, which would understate those countries and reorder the ranking. The start and end figures themselves are in the CSV and PDF.
Did cases actually fall?
The final column draws on a separate dataset: reported {{ disease_name }} cases per 100,000 people across the same years. Coverage and case rates can rise together. Reported case numbers also reflect how actively a country looks for cases, so they are best read as an indication rather than a conclusion.
