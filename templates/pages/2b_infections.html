{# ---- Method note ---- #}

How these figures were calculated

Infection rate
Reported cases ÷ national population × 100,000. A rate, not a raw count, so a large country and a small one can be compared.
Group rate
The whole group’s cases divided by the whole group’s population (population-weighted), not the mean of the country rates — those two differ, and only the first answers “how common is this disease in this group?”.
Data status
“reported” is a normal figure; “reported 0” is a genuine zero; “0 — check” is a zero that looks suspicious in context — it is flagged rather than dropped or trusted. A flagged row is still counted in every total on this page: the source cannot tell a true zero from a year nobody reported, so removing those rows would move each figure on the strength of our own guess.
“Not classified”
Countries with no World-Bank income group in the supplied data. They are kept as a group of their own rather than dropped, for the same reason page 2A keeps territories it cannot match to a region: a country that vanishes from a comparison is worse than one shown as unclassified.
SQL and Python
SQL does the filtering, the rate calculation and the sorting; Python validates every selection against the values the page offers before it reaches the database, then formats the numbers for reading.
