How this ranking was built

Coverage
The figure each country reported to WHO for {{ antigen_name }}. Its denominator is the birth cohort that country targeted, which is what WHO reports as coverage. Change is end minus start, in percentage points (pp). We deliberately do not use doses divided by total population: that is not a coverage rate.
Who is ranked
Only countries with a real coverage figure in both {{ start_year }} and {{ end_year }}. A country missing either year is excluded, so none can appear from nowhere and show an impossible gain. {{ fmt_int(eligible) }} countries qualified.
Coverage above 100%
Kept, not capped. Reported doses can exceed the target cohort, so an end figure above 100 is real and is shown as reported.
Did cases actually fall?
The last columns compare a different dataset: reported cases per 100,000 people for {{ disease_name }}, over the same years. Coverage rising while cases rise is possible, and is shown rather than hidden. Case rates reflect surveillance as much as transmission, so read them as a prompt, not a verdict.
